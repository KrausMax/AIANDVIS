The use of AI made it possible to create a very broad analysis in a short time. With one prompt I got a notebook with 31 visualisations of our model, which I could not have done by hand in the same time. From that I could look at all of them and decide together with my partner which ones were actually interesting.

The first prompt was very general, I only asked for as many visualisations as possible. This gave a lot of breadth, but not every plot was useful and it needed a second step to find out which ones really say something. The depth came afterwards, when we picked specific visualisations and worked on them.

Checking the results against a comparison and against two more training runs with different seeds was very helpful, as it showed which findings hold and which depend on one run. The AI only did this at the second step, so next time I would ask for it from the start.

Not all suggestions were followed. The AI recommended against Fashion-MNIST and suggested FashionCLIP instead, as a group we still went with Fashion-MNIST and ResNet18.

My approach was:
1)  Collecting course information, topic and dataset ideas for the group (no AI)
2)  Deciding on model, task and direction with the group (no AI)
3)  Creating a broad set of Visualizations (AI)
4)  Going through the Visualizations and choosing the important ones with my group (no AI)
5)  Checking the findings and preparing a description of the best Visualizations (AI)
6)  Preparing the slides for the chosen Visualizations (AI), deciding what to show in the presentation (no AI)
